# Insectoid — Visual Head Design

> **Status: APPROVED — simplified visual redesign**
>
> This document defines the visual language of the Insectoid head after the redesign. It does **not** replace or simplify the biological/worldbuilding documentation of the Insectoid race. The redesign changes only how the race is visually represented.

## Core visual concept

The Insectoid head should read primarily as:

> **A humanoid head with clearly insectoid facial features.**

It should **not** read as:

> A realistic insect enlarged and transformed into a humanoid.

The visual language is deliberately simplified so the race can be generated consistently and remain stylistically compatible with the other humanoidal races of the world.

## Primary identifying features

### 1. Large compound eyes

Large compound eyes are one of the strongest visual identifiers of the race.

- Large relative to the head.
- Clearly insectoid.
- Faceted/compound appearance may be visible.
- Simple, readable overall shape.
- Highly recognizable even at a distance.
- Positioned in a character-friendly way rather than reproducing complex real-insect anatomy.
- Eye color may vary between individuals and lines.

The eyes should provide much of the insectoid identity without requiring additional anatomical detail.

### 2. No human nose

The face has:

- no human-like nose,
- no pronounced nasal bridge,
- no human nostrils as a facial feature.

The central facial area should remain clean and simple.

### 3. No human mouth

The Insectoid has no conventional humanoid mouth.

- No human lips.
- No conventional human mouth line.
- The lower face is formed by the insectoid jaw/mandible structure.

Adding a normal human mouth should be avoided because it weakens the intended visual identity.

## 4. Extremely wide-opening jaw

The jaw is one of the defining features of the redesign.

### Closed / neutral

When closed, the mandibles form a clean, readable insectoid lower-face structure without excessive mouthpart complexity.

### Open

The jaw can open **extremely widely**.

The visual impression should approach:

> **A jaw opening almost from one side of the head to the other — an insectoid “smile from ear to ear”.**

The opening is deliberately much wider than a human mouth and should remain visually clean in the Cartoon style.

The wide opening is a characteristic of the race, not a requirement to display realistic arthropod anatomy.

## 5. Simplified mandibles

The mandibles should be:

- clearly insectoid,
- large enough to be immediately recognizable,
- simple in construction,
- easy to reproduce consistently,
- integrated into the humanoid head silhouette.

Avoid realistic multi-part insect mouthparts.

The mandibles should function primarily as a strong graphic shape.

## 6. Antennae

The head has a pair of simple antennae.

Characteristics:

- two antennae,
- short to moderate length,
- thin and clean,
- simple silhouette,
- preferably one or two uncomplicated bends,
- optional small tips,
- capable of subtle movement and expression.

Avoid highly branched or anatomically complex antennae.

They should be recognizable without becoming the dominant element of the head.

## 7. Head shape

The head may be slightly larger than a human head.

The general head volume should remain relatively humanoidal:

- clear front silhouette,
- clear three-quarter silhouette,
- readable profile,
- smooth major shapes,
- limited number of armor plates,
- no excessive segmentation.

The head should look like a coherent character head, not a detailed insect carapace.

## 8. Simplified exoskeleton

The head may use a simplified exoskeletal outer surface.

- Plates should be large and simple.
- Segmentation should be subtle.
- Decorative divisions are preferred over anatomical complexity.
- The number of visible plates should remain low.
- Dense networks of insect anatomy should not be shown.

The exoskeleton supports silhouette and color design rather than becoming the subject of the illustration.

## 9. Facial expression

The simplified anatomy must still allow clear character expression.

Useful expression states include:

- neutral,
- curious,
- tired,
- happy,
- aggressive,
- surprised.

Expression should primarily be communicated through:

- eye orientation and shape,
- antenna position,
- jaw opening,
- head tilt.

The design should not depend on human eyebrows or human lips.

## 10. Cartoon style

Preferred visual style: **Cartoon**.

Required qualities:

- clean outlines,
- simple readable shapes,
- limited detail,
- flat colors with light shading,
- strong silhouette,
- good readability from a distance,
- expressive face,
- consistent proportions,
- high repeatability between generations.

The head should remain understandable even when rendered relatively small.

## 11. What the redesign deliberately removes

The visual redesign should **not** show:

- realistic insect anatomy,
- realistic insect mouthpart systems,
- excessive exoskeleton segmentation,
- numerous tiny anatomical structures,
- complex mandible mechanisms,
- insect-like facial anatomy covering the whole head,
- unnecessary biological detail,
- decorative details whose main purpose is to demonstrate the underlying biology.

These elements may remain fully documented in the biological/worldbuilding files.

## 12. Visual priority order

When generation has to simplify the design, preserve features in this order:

1. **Large compound eyes**
2. **Extremely wide insectoid jaw / mandibles**
3. **Absence of human nose and human mouth**
4. **Two simple antennae**
5. **Humanoid head proportions**
6. **Simplified exoskeleton**
7. Minor decorative or lineage-specific details

The first four characteristics should remain recognizable in virtually every standard Insectoid depiction.

## 13. Design philosophy

This redesign is a **visual simplification only**.

The Insectoid race remains biologically complex in the world documentation. The illustration does not need to communicate every biological feature.

The intended visual shorthand is:

> **Large compound eyes + simple antennae + extremely wide insectoid jaw + humanoid head.**

This combination should be sufficient to identify an Insectoid without requiring a realistic depiction of an arthropod.

## 14. Approved direction

The approved concept establishes:

- humanoidal head volume,
- oversized compound eyes,
- clean central facial structure,
- no human nose,
- no human lips,
- large simplified mandibles,
- extremely wide jaw opening,
- simple antennae,
- simplified exoskeletal plates,
- Cartoon rendering,
- limited anatomical detail.

This visual language is the basis for the next stage of the Insectoid redesign.

## 15. Scope

This file defines **only the visual head redesign**.

It does not yet define:

- the full Insectoid body,
- Worker visual design,
- Scout visual design,
- T-POSE card,
- line-specific body anatomy,
- biological rules of the species.

These should be developed separately while preserving the approved head language above.
